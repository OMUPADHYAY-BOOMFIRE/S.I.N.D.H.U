Deep-Dive Analysis: Pros & Cons

Mito-OceanNet (Competitor)

Pros:

Irregular Boundary Handling: Graph representation natively handles complex coastline topologies and island masks without wasting compute on land pixels.

Dynamic Connectivity: Explicitly models physical ocean transport (ocean currents) through directional graph edges rather than fixed Euclidean convolutions.

Compute Efficiency: Sparse expert routing (MoE) and prototype memory (MitoCode) avoid running heavy networks on stable, predictable water masses.

Explicit Uncertainty: Heteroscedastic head provides per-pixel, per-depth uncertainty estimates in a single forward pass.

Cons:

Lack of Predictive Capability: Focuses purely on same-day reconstruction (t \to t), omitting true multi-week forecasting (t \to t+\ell).

High Output Dimensionality: Directly predicting 15 depth channels increases decoder parameter count and can lead to vertical profile inconsistencies.

Complex Graph Overhead: Dynamic graph construction and sparse routing introduce high CPU/GPU synchronization latencies on standard hardware.

Macro-Climate Blindness: Lacks explicit conditioning on basin-wide climate modes like the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) or ENSO.

Our Architecture (PDF Document)

Pros:

True Multi-Week Forecasting: Predicts ocean thermal structure up to 30 days in advance, providing much higher operational value for marine navigation and cyclone forecasting.

Vertical EOF Dimensionality Reduction: Compressing 15 depth levels into 5 principal EOF modes enforces physical vertical continuity, speeds up convergence, and dramatically reduces parameter space.

FILM Macro-Climate Conditioning: Explicitly informs the network of large-scale climate drivers (IOD / Niño 3.4) and target lead time \ell, allowing a single model to dynamically adjust physics across climate regimes.

Physically Grounded Loss: Mixed Layer Depth (MLD) gating ensures the model does not enforce artificial smoothness in turbulent upper layers where mixing occurs naturally.

Cons:

Euclidean Grid Limitations: Standard 2D convolutions struggle with boundary conditions near jagged coastlines and complex island archipelagos.

Uniform Computation: Processes every grid cell (from quiescent open ocean to chaotic coastal eddies) through identical convolutional layers, wasting FLOPs.

No Explicit Prototype Memory: Re-computes recurring ocean states from scratch rather than retrieving historical ocean-state templates.

Module Breakdown for Human Comprehension
Module 1: Ingestion & Grid Harmonization
The architecture receives two distinct data streams. The physical stream pulls 23 surface variables (temperature, salinity, sea level anomaly, currents) over a 21-frame historical window to capture major ocean cycles (like the Madden-Julian Oscillation). The macro-climate stream ingests the current Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) state, the ENSO (Niño 3.4) state, and the specific number of days into the future the user wants to predict (the lead time \ell). All land masses are mathematically masked out to save computational resources.
Module 2: Adaptive Spatial Graph Processing
Instead of processing the ocean as a rigid picture, the model converts valid ocean grid cells into interconnected nodes. The connections (edges) between these nodes are formed dynamically based on actual ocean currents, distance, and feature similarity. An "ATP Compute Gate" evaluates how complex or novel a specific ocean region is. Based on this demand, data is routed to specialized parallel expert networks (e.g., a Thermal expert for surface heat, a Transport expert for strong currents) rather than forcing every drop of water through the exact same mathematical equations. The graph state is then transformed back into a grid format for time-based forecasting.
Module 3: FILM-Conditioned Temporal Forecasting
This is where the climate indices actively steer the prediction. The macro-climate stream is processed by a small neural network to generate affine transformation vectors (\gamma and \beta). These vectors use Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FILM) to directly adjust the inner workings of the ConvLSTM (or Cuboid-Attention) sequence model. This means the exact same model will dynamically alter its internal physics depending on whether it is predicting 7 days ahead during an active IOD event, or 30 days ahead during a neutral climate phase.
Module 4: EOF Compression & Physical Decoding
To prevent the model from guessing physically impossible temperature jumps between depths, it does not predict the 15 depth levels directly. Instead, the decoder predicts just 5 Empirical Orthogonal Function (EOF) spatial coefficient maps. These 5 maps represent the core structural "modes" of the ocean. They are mathematically multiplied against a fixed basis matrix (\Phi) created during training, instantly expanding the 5 modes out into a complete, mathematically sound vertical temperature profile extending from the surface down to 1000\,\text{m}.
Module 5: Reconstruction & Physics-Gated Loss
The expanded temperature profile is subjected to a final physics checkpoint during training. A Mixed Layer Depth (MLD) gate calculates the depth of the turbulent surface layer. It allows the temperature to vary freely within this chaotic mixed layer but strictly penalizes unphysical temperature spikes in the deeper, stable ocean below it. Simultaneously, a GEBCO bathymetry mask ensures the model does not try to predict ocean temperatures underground where the seafloor rises. Every prediction is paired with a calibrated uncertainty score (heteroscedastic likelihood) and validated against held-out, independent ARGO float sensors.

"""

    1. Dynamic Physical Ocean Graph Net (Advective GNN)

    6. MLD-Gated Physics Loss & GEBCO Bathymetry Masking

import math
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
from typing import Tuple, Dict, Optional


# ==============================================================================
# MODULE 1: DYNAMIC ADVECTIVE OCEAN GRAPH CONVERTER
# ==============================================================================
class DynamicOceanGraph(nn.Module):
    """
    Transforms regular 2D ocean grids into a dynamic sparse graph where edge
    weights and message vectors are determined by surface advection velocities (u, v).
    """
    def __init__(self, in_channels: int, node_dim: int, k_neighbors: int = 8):
        super().__init__()
        self.k_neighbors = k_neighbors
        self.node_proj = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(in_channels, node_dim, kernel_size=1),
            nn.GroupNorm(4, node_dim),
            nn.GELU()
        )
        self.edge_mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(2 + 2, node_dim),  # [spatial_dist(dx, dy) + transport_vector(u, v)]
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Linear(node_dim, 1),
            nn.Sigmoid()
        )

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor, u_v: torch.Tensor, ocean_mask: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """
        x: [B, C, H, W] Surface observations
        u_v: [B, 2, H, W] Surface Current vectors (u_o, v_o)
        ocean_mask: [B, 1, H, W] Boolean mask (1 = ocean, 0 = land)
        """
        B, C, H, W = x.shape
        proj = self.node_proj(x) * ocean_mask  # [B, node_dim, H, W]
        
        # In multi-year distributed runs, graph adjacency is constructed via spatial KNN.
        # For localized inference and 3-year Colab optimization, we employ directional 
        # depthwise spatial graph convolutions weighted by the advection vector field:
        u_vel = u_v[:, 0:1, :, :]
        v_vel = u_v[:, 1:2, :, :]
        
        # Directional shift simulating advective edge routing
        shifted_north = F.pad(proj[:, :, 1:, :], (0, 0, 0, 1)) * F.relu(v_vel)
        shifted_south = F.pad(proj[:, :, :-1, :], (0, 0, 1, 0)) * F.relu(-v_vel)
        shifted_east  = F.pad(proj[:, :, :, :-1], (1, 0, 0, 0)) * F.relu(u_vel)
        shifted_west  = F.pad(proj[:, :, :, 1:], (0, 1, 0, 0)) * F.relu(-u_vel)
        
        graph_context = proj + (shifted_north + shifted_south + shifted_east + shifted_west) * 0.25
        return graph_context


# ==============================================================================
# MODULE 2: ATP-GATED SPARSE MIXTURE OF EXPERTS (MoE)
# ==============================================================================
class OceanExpert(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim: int, expert_type: str):
        super().__init__()
        self.expert_type = expert_type
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(dim, dim, kernel_size=3, padding=1, groups=1),
            nn.GroupNorm(4, dim),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Conv2d(dim, dim, kernel_size=3, padding=1),
            nn.GroupNorm(4, dim)
        )
        
    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        return x + self.net(x)


class ATPSparseMoE(nn.Module):
    """
    Computes local thermodynamic turbulence / novelty (Compute Demand 'ATP')
    and routes node features through specialized physical experts.
    """
    def __init__(self, dim: int, num_experts: int = 3):
        super().__init__()
        self.num_experts = num_experts
        # Experts: 0 = Thermal Stratification, 1 = Dynamic Advection, 2 = Deep Boundary
        self.experts = nn.ModuleList([
            OceanExpert(dim, expert_type="thermal"),
            OceanExpert(dim, expert_type="advection"),
            OceanExpert(dim, expert_type="boundary")
        ])
        self.gate = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(dim, dim // 2, kernel_size=1),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Conv2d(dim // 2, num_experts, kernel_size=1),
            nn.Softmax(dim=1)
        )

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
        # Compute gating demand scores
        gate_weights = self.gate(x)  # [B, num_experts, H, W]
        
        # Parallel expert computation
        out = torch.zeros_like(x)
        for i, expert in enumerate(self.experts):
            weight = gate_weights[:, i:i+1, :, :]
            out = out + (weight * expert(x))
            
        return out, gate_weights


# ==============================================================================
# MODULE 3: MACRO-CLIMATE & LEAD-TIME FiLM MODULATOR
# ==============================================================================
class ClimateFiLM(nn.Module):
    """
    Feature-wise Linear Modulation:
    Generates dynamic channel-wise scale (gamma) and shift (beta) based on:
      - Dipole Mode Index (IOD / DMI)
      - ENSO (Niño 3.4 Index)
      - Continuous Forecast Lead-Time (ℓ ∈ [7, 30] days)
    """
    def __init__(self, climate_dim: int, target_channels: int):
        super().__init__()
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(climate_dim, 64),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Linear(64, target_channels * 2)
        )

    def forward(self, climate_vector: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
        """
        climate_vector: [B, 3] -> (IOD_DMI, Nino34, LeadTime / 30.0)
        """
        params = self.mlp(climate_vector)  # [B, 2 * target_channels]
        gamma, beta = torch.chunk(params, 2, dim=-1)
        # Reshape for broadcasting over [B, C, H, W]
        gamma = gamma.unsqueeze(-1).unsqueeze(-1) + 1.0  # Centered at 1
        beta = beta.unsqueeze(-1).unsqueeze(-1)           # Centered at 0
        return gamma, beta


# ==============================================================================
# MODULE 4: FiLM-MODULATED SPATIOTEMPORAL ConvLSTM CELL
# ==============================================================================
class FiLMConvLSTMCell(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, input_dim: int, hidden_dim: int, kernel_size: int = 3):
        super().__init__()
        self.input_dim = input_dim
        self.hidden_dim = hidden_dim
        self.padding = kernel_size // 2
        
        self.conv = nn.Conv2d(
            in_channels=input_dim + hidden_dim,
            out_channels=4 * hidden_dim,
            kernel_size=kernel_size,
            padding=self.padding
        )

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor, h_prev: torch.Tensor, c_prev: torch.Tensor, 
                gamma: torch.Tensor, beta: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
        combined = torch.cat([x, h_prev], dim=1)
        gates = self.conv(combined)
        
        # Inject Macro-Climate Modulation across internal recurrent projections
        gates = (gamma.expand_as(gates) * gates) + beta.expand_as(gates)
        
        i, f, o, g = torch.split(gates, self.hidden_dim, dim=1)
        i = torch.sigmoid(i)
        f = torch.sigmoid(f)
        o = torch.sigmoid(o)
        g = torch.tanh(g)
        
        c_cur = f * c_prev + i * g
        h_cur = o * torch.tanh(c_cur)
        return h_cur, c_cur


# ==============================================================================
# MODULE 5: 5-MODE VERTICAL EOF DECODER & UNCERTAINTY HEAD
# ==============================================================================
class VerticalEOFDecoder(nn.Module):
    """
    Projects spatiotemporal latent features into 5 spatial EOF mode coefficient
    maps (c_k). These maps are expanded into 15 physical depth levels using the
    fixed, training-derived SVD EOF basis matrix Phi ∈ R^{5 x 15}.
    """
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim: int, num_modes: int = 5, num_depths: int = 15):
        super().__init__()
        self.num_modes = num_modes
        self.num_depths = num_depths
        
        # Spatial Mode Generator (outputs mode coefficients c_k)
        self.mode_conv = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(hidden_dim, 64, kernel_size=3, padding=1),
            nn.GroupNorm(8, 64),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Conv2d(64, num_modes, kernel_size=1)
        )
        
        # Heteroscedastic Aleatoric Uncertainty Head (Predicts log-variance sigma^2 per depth)
        self.uncertainty_conv = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv2d(hidden_dim, 32, kernel_size=3, padding=1),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Conv2d(32, num_depths, kernel_size=1)
        )
        
        # Fixed EOF Eigenvector Basis Matrix (Phi) & Climatological Mean (Initialized as buffers)
        self.register_buffer("eof_basis", torch.zeros(num_modes, num_depths))
        self.register_buffer("climatology_mean", torch.zeros(1, num_depths, 1, 1))

    def load_precomputed_basis(self, basis: torch.Tensor, mean: torch.Tensor):
        """Loads fixed EOF basis matrix computed via 20-year GLORYS SVD analysis."""
        self.eof_basis.copy_(basis)
        self.climatology_mean.copy_(mean.view(1, self.num_depths, 1, 1))

    def forward(self, h: torch.Tensor) -> Tuple[torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor, torch.Tensor]:
        B, _, H, W = h.shape
        
        # 1. Predict 5 spatial coefficient maps: [B, 5, H, W]
        c_k = self.mode_conv(h)
        
        # 2. Reshape for vertical reconstruction matrix multiplication:
        # c_k_flat: [B, H*W, 5]
        c_k_flat = c_k.permute(0, 2, 3, 1).reshape(B * H * W, self.num_modes)
        
        # T_anom_flat = c_k_flat @ eof_basis: [B * H * W, 15]
        t_anom_flat = torch.matmul(c_k_flat, self.eof_basis)
        
        # Reshape to 3D Field: [B, 15, H, W]
        t_anom = t_anom_flat.view(B, H, W, self.num_depths).permute(0, 3, 1, 2)
        
        # Add climatological baseline mean: T(x, y, d) = T_mean(d) + Σ (c_k * Φ_k)
        t_recon = t_anom + self.climatology_mean
        
        # 3. Predict Aleatoric Uncertainty (log(sigma^2))
        log_var = self.uncertainty_conv(h)
        
        return t_recon, c_k, log_var


# ==============================================================================
# MASTER INTEGRATED HYBRID PIPELINE
# ==============================================================================
class HydroGraphFILMEngine(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, in_channels: int = 7, hidden_dim: int = 64, num_modes: int = 5):
        super().__init__()
        self.in_channels = in_channels
        self.hidden_dim = hidden_dim
        
        # Sub-modules
        self.graph_builder = DynamicOceanGraph(in_channels=in_channels, node_dim=hidden_dim)
        self.moe = ATPSparseMoE(dim=hidden_dim, num_experts=3)
        self.film_gen = ClimateFiLM(climate_dim=3, target_channels=hidden_dim * 4)
        self.convlstm_cell = FiLMConvLSTMCell(input_dim=hidden_dim, hidden_dim=hidden_dim)
        self.decoder = VerticalEOFDecoder(hidden_dim=hidden_dim, num_modes=num_modes, num_depths=15)

    def forward(self, seq_x: torch.Tensor, seq_uv: torch.Tensor, 
                climate_vec: torch.Tensor, ocean_mask: torch.Tensor) -> Dict[str, torch.Tensor]:
        """
        seq_x: [B, T, C, H, W] Sequence of surface states (T = 7..21 frames)
        seq_uv: [B, T, 2, H, W] Surface Current vectors (u, v)
        climate_vec: [B, 3] (IOD, Nino34, LeadTime_l)
        ocean_mask: [B, 1, H, W] Land/Sea boundary mask
        """
        B, T, C, H, W = seq_x.shape
        h = torch.zeros(B, self.hidden_dim, H, W, device=seq_x.device)
        c = torch.zeros(B, self.hidden_dim, H, W, device=seq_x.device)
        
        # Generate FiLM affine vectors from scalar climate modes + forecast horizon
        gamma, beta = self.film_gen(climate_vec)
        
        # Spatiotemporal sequence roll-out
        for t in range(T):
            x_t = seq_x[:, t]
            uv_t = seq_uv[:, t]
            
            # Step 1: Spatial Dynamic Graph Propagation
            g_t = self.graph_builder(x_t, uv_t, ocean_mask)
            
            # Step 2: ATP Gated Sparse Expert Processing
            moe_t, gate_weights = self.moe(g_t)
            
            # Step 3: FiLM-Gated Temporal Propagation
            h, c = self.convlstm_cell(moe_t, h, c, gamma, beta)
            
        # Step 4: Vertical EOF Expansion & Uncertainty Decoding
        t_pred, mode_maps, log_var = self.decoder(h)
        
        # Enforce Land Mask
        t_pred = t_pred * ocean_mask
        
        return {
            "temperature_3d": t_pred,
            "mode_coefficients": mode_maps,
            "log_variance": log_var,
            "atp_gates": gate_weights
        }

# MODULE 6: PHYSICS-GATED LOSS FUNCTIONS
# ==============================================================================
class PhysicsGatedLoss(nn.Module):
    """
    Enforces oceanographic physical laws:
      1. Heteroscedastic Likelihood Loss (trains predictive confidence sigma)
      2. MLD-Gated Vertical Smoothness (prohibits unphysical inversions below mixed layer)
      3. GEBCO Sub-Bottom Topography Masking (zeroes subterranean predictions)
    """
    def __init__(self, alpha_recon: float = 1.0, alpha_phys: float = 0.15):
        super().__init__()
        self.alpha_recon = alpha_recon
        self.alpha_phys = alpha_phys
        # 15 mandatory depths in meters:
        self.register_buffer("depths", torch.tensor([
            0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 200, 300, 500, 700, 1000
        ], dtype=torch.float32))

    def forward(self, t_pred: torch.Tensor, t_true: torch.Tensor, 
                log_var: torch.Tensor, mld: torch.Tensor, 
                gebco_bathymetry: torch.Tensor, mask: torch.Tensor) -> Dict[str, torch.Tensor]:
        """
        mld: [B, 1, H, W] Mixed Layer Depth (meters)
        gebco_bathymetry: [B, 1, H, W] Seafloor depth (meters, negative or positive downward)
        """
        # 1. Mask Out Subterranean Cells (GEBCO enforcement)
        # depth_grid: [1, 15, 1, 1]
        depth_grid = self.depths.view(1, 15, 1, 1).to(t_pred.device)
        is_water = (depth_grid <= gebco_bathymetry) & (mask > 0)
        
        # 2. Heteroscedastic Regression Loss (Gaussian NLL)
        precision = torch.exp(-log_var)
        mse_residual = (t_pred - t_true) ** 2
        recon_loss = 0.5 * (precision * mse_residual + log_var)
        recon_loss = (recon_loss * is_water).sum() / (is_water.sum() + 1e-6)
        
        # 3. MLD-Gated Vertical Smoothness Loss
        # In upper layers (d <= MLD), turbulent mixing allows temperature variations.
        # Below MLD (d > MLD), temperature must decay monotonically: dT/dz <= 0.
        dt_dz = t_pred[:, 1:, :, :] - t_pred[:, :-1, :, :]  # Forward difference across depth
        below_mld = (depth_grid[:, 1:, :, :] > mld) & is_water[:, 1:, :, :]
        
        # Penalize positive thermal jumps (inversions) in stable deep strata
        unphysical_inversion = F.relu(dt_dz)
        phys_loss = (unphysical_inversion * below_mld).sum() / (below_mld.sum() + 1e-6)
        
        total_loss = self.alpha_recon * recon_loss + self.alpha_phys * phys_loss
        
        return {
            "total_loss": total_loss,
            "recon_nll": recon_loss,
            "physics_inversion_loss": phys_loss
        }
